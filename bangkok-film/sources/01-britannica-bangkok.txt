Bangkok, capital and chief port of Thailand. It is the only cosmopolitan city in a country of small towns and villages and is Thailand's cultural and commercial centre. Bangkok is located on the delta of the Chao Phraya River, about 25 miles (40 km) north of the Gulf of Thailand.

Founding and History:
Bangkok was formerly divided into two municipalities: Krung Thep on the east bank and Thon Buri on the west. In 1782, General Chao Phraya Chakkri assumed the throne as King Rama I, the founder of the Chakri dynasty. King Rama I moved the royal capital across the Chao Phraya River from Thonburi to the east bank at Rattanakosin Island, where the river's broad westward bend served as a natural defensive moat against invasion.

Monuments and Royal Architecture:
In 1782, King Rama I began construction of the Grand Palace, a magnificent walled royal complex that served as the official and ceremonial residence of the kings of Siam. Within the Grand Palace compound stands Wat Phra Kaew, also known as the Temple of the Emerald Buddha, the most sacred Buddhist temple in Thailand, housing the revered Emerald Buddha figure carved from jasper.

Adjacent to the Grand Palace lies Wat Pho, the Temple of the Reclining Buddha, one of the oldest and largest temples in Bangkok, renowned for its giant gilded reclining Buddha statue and as the birthplace of traditional Thai massage. Across the Chao Phraya River on the Thonburi bank stands Wat Arun, the Temple of Dawn, whose striking porcelain-encrusted central prang rises steeply above the riverbank.

Modern Metropolis:
Throughout the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, under King Mongkut (Rama IV) and King Chulalongkorn (Rama V), modern roads, canals, and railways transformed Bangkok into the premier metropolis of Southeast Asia. Today, Bangkok remains the political, economic, and spiritual heart of the Kingdom of Thailand.
